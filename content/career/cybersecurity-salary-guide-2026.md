## Cybersecurity salary: the short answer

In the United States, the median annual wage for **information security analysts** was **$129,180 in May 2025**, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). The lowest-paid 10 percent earned less than **$75,090**, while the highest-paid 10 percent earned more than **$199,850**. These are national figures for one BLS occupation—not a guaranteed salary, an entry-level offer, or a single average for every cybersecurity job.

That distinction matters. “Cybersecurity” includes SOC analysts, penetration testers, GRC specialists, security engineers, incident responders, cloud security teams, and management roles. Their responsibilities, experience requirements, and pay differ. This guide explains how to read the available salary data, compare opportunities, and assess an offer. It focuses on the U.S. market; pay elsewhere depends on local labor markets, currency, tax, and employment norms.

If you are comparing roles as well as pay, start with our guide to [cybersecurity job titles and career paths](/career/cybersecurity-jobs/).

## U.S. information security analyst salary benchmarks

The [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm) reports these national wage figures for information security analysts:

| Measure | Annual wage | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| 10th percentile | Less than $75,090 | 10% of workers earned below this amount |
| Median | $129,180 | Half earned more and half earned less |
| 90th percentile | More than $199,850 | 10% of workers earned above this amount |

These wages are from **May 2025**, the latest annual estimates available as of October 2026. They are not a 2026 salary survey and do not predict what a specific employer will pay. The BLS projects 21% employment growth for information security analysts from 2025 to 2035, with about 14,100 openings each year. Job growth does not guarantee a particular salary or make every opening entry-level.

### Pay by industry

The same BLS profile reports different medians across major industries employing information security analysts:

| Industry | Median annual wage, May 2025 |
|---|---:|
| Information | $138,650 |
| Computer systems design and related services | $132,410 |
| Finance and insurance | $130,630 |
| Management of companies and enterprises | $128,950 |
| Management, scientific, and technical consulting services | $125,420 |

Industry medians describe workers across experience levels. They are useful context when comparing employers, but they do not mean every finance job pays more than every consulting job. Role scope, location, seniority, employer size, and the compensation mix all matter.

## Cybersecurity salary by role

There is no single authoritative government wage category for every modern security title. The BLS figure above applies to information security analysts, while titles such as cloud security engineer or penetration tester may be classified differently depending on their actual duties. Use the table below as a guide to what to compare—not as fabricated salary bands.

| Role | What to compare in salary data and job offers |
|---|---|
| SOC or security analyst | Shift differential, on-call rotation, incident scope, SIEM and endpoint responsibilities |
| Incident response / DFIR | Incident command duties, travel or call-out requirements, forensic specialization, and experience level |
| Penetration tester | Consulting utilization targets, report and client responsibilities, travel, certifications, and whether compensation includes billable bonuses |
| GRC analyst | Regulatory scope, audit ownership, client-facing work, industry specialization, and progression to risk leadership |
| Cloud security engineer | Cloud platform depth, infrastructure-as-code, architecture ownership, and whether the role includes software engineering |
| Application security engineer | Secure design and code-review expectations, product ownership, developer enablement, and engineering level |
| Security architect or manager | Organization size, budget and staff responsibility, decision authority, and total compensation rather than base salary alone |

When salary sites report a figure for one of these titles, check the sample size, date, country, seniority, and whether it is base salary or total compensation. Small samples and inconsistent job titles can produce large differences between websites.

## Cybersecurity salary by experience level

Experience changes the work an employer expects you to own. Entry-level staff may triage alerts, document findings, and follow established procedures. Experienced specialists may lead investigations, design controls, build detection systems, or advise product and infrastructure teams. Leadership roles add responsibility for budgets, teams, risk decisions, and business outcomes.

But do not treat a percentile as a career stage. The BLS 10th percentile is not a published “entry-level cybersecurity salary,” and its 90th percentile is not a standard senior salary. Those thresholds include workers from different locations, industries, and employment settings. For a realistic comparison:

1. Compare postings with similar responsibilities and years of experience.
2. Separate base pay from bonus, equity, benefits, and on-call pay.
3. Check local BLS wage data for the occupation and geographic area.
4. Treat entry-level postings as their own market; do not anchor on a national median that includes experienced workers.

## Cybersecurity salary by state and city

Location affects salary because employers compete in different labor markets and face different costs, industry mixes, and hiring needs. A national median can hide wide differences between metropolitan areas. Remote jobs do not always pay a single national rate: some employers adjust compensation by employee location, while others use broader bands.

Use the BLS [state wage estimates](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oessrcst.htm) and [metropolitan-area wage estimates](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oessrcma.htm) to compare information security analyst wages near you. Check the year and occupation title before drawing conclusions. For current opportunities, also compare live job postings in the location where you can legally work.

When comparing two cities, look beyond gross salary. Estimate housing and transport costs, state and local taxes, health insurance contributions, paid time off, retirement contributions, and whether you would need to relocate or commute. A higher nominal offer may leave you with less disposable income after these costs.

## What can increase cybersecurity pay?

Pay tends to follow the value, scarcity, and scope of the work—not the number of tools or certifications on a résumé. Factors that can influence an offer include:

- **Role scope:** owning architecture, incident decisions, or security outcomes usually carries different responsibility from following a defined queue.
- **Relevant experience:** production experience in IT, software, cloud, identity, or security operations can reduce ramp-up time.
- **Specialized skills:** cloud architecture, application security, incident leadership, detection engineering, and risk expertise may be valuable when they match an employer's needs.
- **Industry and employer:** regulated, high-availability, or large-scale environments may have distinct responsibilities and compensation practices.
- **Location and work model:** local wage competition, remote pay policies, relocation, and clearance eligibility can affect compensation.
- **Communication and leadership:** explaining risk, writing clear recommendations, and coordinating fixes can expand a professional's impact.

These are factors to investigate, not guarantees of a pay premium. A certification may help meet a job requirement, but it does not automatically produce a raise. ## Base salary is only part of compensation

Before accepting an offer, understand the full package and the conditions attached to it. Ask the recruiter or hiring manager to clarify:

- **Base salary:** annual rate, pay frequency, and whether the role is exempt or eligible for overtime.
- **Bonus:** target amount, how it is calculated, and whether it is discretionary or tied to measurable goals.
- **Equity:** grant type, vesting schedule, and what happens if you leave; private-company equity may not be liquid.
- **Benefits:** health premiums, retirement match, paid leave, education budget, and equipment.
- **On-call and shift work:** rotation frequency, response expectations, and compensation or time off.
- **Work location:** remote eligibility, geographic pay band, office days, and travel.
- **Role expectations:** team size, incident authority, tools, training, and the first six-month objectives.

Compare the written offer, not just a verbal headline. If a recruiter quotes total compensation, ask for each component separately so you can compare it with other offers on the same basis.

## How to negotiate a cybersecurity salary

Negotiation is easier when you can show how your experience matches the role and understand the employer's pay range. Prepare before discussing a number:

1. **Research comparable roles.** Use BLS data for broad benchmarks and current job postings for title, location, and seniority context.
2. **Make a short evidence list.** Connect your skills and outcomes to the responsibilities in the job description. Do not overstate lab work as production experience.
3. **Ask about the range.** You can ask for the approved salary band and how the employer places candidates within it.
4. **Discuss the whole package.** If base pay is fixed, ask whether there is flexibility in sign-on bonus, review timing, training budget, or remote arrangements.
5. **Get the details in writing.** Confirm salary, bonus terms, benefits, location, and start date before accepting.

A concise response might be: “Based on the scope of the role and my experience with [relevant skill or responsibility], I was targeting a base range of [researched range]. Is there flexibility in the base salary or the overall package?” Use a range supported by local data and your alternatives; do not choose one solely from a national median.

## A practical plan to improve your earning potential

If your goal is higher pay, begin by targeting a role rather than chasing an abstract “cybersecurity salary.”

### Step 1: Pick a direction

Choose a target such as SOC operations, GRC, IAM, cloud security, application security, or penetration testing. Compare the actual work and requirements using our [cybersecurity job paths overview](/career/cybersecurity-jobs/).

### Step 2: Study real postings

Collect 15–20 relevant job listings in the location where you can work. Note recurring responsibilities, required experience, tools, certifications, and salary ranges where employers publish them. Ignore requirements that appear only once until you confirm they are essential.

### Step 3: Build evidence for the gap

Create one project or work sample that demonstrates a relevant task. Examples include a documented lab incident investigation, a detection rule with test data, a cloud least-privilege design, or a fictional risk assessment. Explain your method and limits honestly.

### Step 4: Compare compensation regularly

Review new postings and official wage data every few months. Keep a record of your responsibilities and measurable outcomes so you have evidence for performance reviews and future interviews. A role change may improve pay, but compare learning, stability, benefits, and working conditions as well.

## Frequently asked questions about cybersecurity salary

**What is the average cybersecurity salary in the U.S.?**

There is no single average for all cybersecurity roles. The BLS reports a $129,180 median annual wage for information security analysts in May 2025. That is a median for one occupation, not an average for every security job.

**How much does an entry-level cybersecurity analyst make?**

The BLS does not publish that median as an entry-level-specific figure. Entry-level pay depends on location, role scope, relevant experience, and employer. Compare junior postings in your area and do not assume the overall occupation median is a starting salary.

**Which cybersecurity job pays the most?**

There is no reliable universal ranking because titles and survey samples differ. Senior architects, managers, and specialized engineers may have higher compensation, but their scope, experience requirements, and total-pay structure differ. Compare role-matched data and written offers.

**Does a cybersecurity certification increase salary?**

A certification can help qualify you for some postings or demonstrate focused knowledge. It does not guarantee a raise. Its value depends on whether employers for your target role request it and whether you can apply the knowledge at work.

**Do remote cybersecurity jobs pay less?**

There is no universal rule. Some employers use location-based salary bands, while others use a broader national range. Ask how the organization sets pay for remote employees and compare offers in the same geographic market.

**Is cybersecurity a high-paying career?**

Some cybersecurity occupations pay well relative to the wider labor market, but compensation varies substantially. The BLS information security analyst median was higher than the median for all occupations in May 2025. That comparison does not mean every cybersecurity job is highly paid or that a new entrant will earn the occupation median.
