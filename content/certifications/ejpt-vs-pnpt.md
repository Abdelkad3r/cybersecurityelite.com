Welcome to a **CyberSecurity Elite** honest breakdown of **eJPT vs PNPT** — the two certifications most people weigh when they want their first *practical* penetration testing credential rather than another multiple-choice knowledge exam. Here is the short verdict up front so you can stop scrolling if that is all you need: **take the eJPT first** to build confidence and fundamentals cheaply with no report to worry about, then **level up to the PNPT** for real Active Directory work, a professional report, and a live debrief that actually moves a resume. Both come from respected vendors, both are fully hands-on, and they solve different problems at different stages. The rest of this guide explains exactly why, and when your situation might justify skipping straight to the PNPT.

> **Confirm current details before you buy.** Exam formats, lab time, retake policies, and pricing all change. The figures below are approximate 2026 ballparks — verify everything on INE Security's official eJPT page and TCM Security's official PNPT page before purchasing.

This table is the centerpiece. If you read nothing else, read this.

Two certs, two philosophies. The eJPT proves you can *find and exploit* things in a lab. The PNPT proves you can *run an engagement end to end* and communicate it like a consultant.

## eJPT deep dive

### What it is

The **eJPT** — eLearnSecurity Junior Penetration Tester — is now delivered by **INE Security**. It is a fully practical, **beginner-level** exam designed for people who have some IT or networking familiarity but are new to offensive security. There is no essay writing and no memorization gauntlet; you are dropped into a lab and expected to actually do the work.

### Format

You get a target lab environment and a set of **dynamic multiple-choice questions** — commonly around 35. The trick that makes it practical rather than a trivia quiz: the *correct answers depend on what you actually discover and exploit in the lab*. You cannot bluff "what version is the service on host X" or "which credential unlocks the share" without going in and getting the data yourself. The exam window is generous (multiple days; often cited around 48 hours — verify), so time pressure is low and this is a low-stress first exam. **Crucially, there is no written report.**

### What it covers

- Basic network and host enumeration
- Simple exploitation and getting a foothold
- Basic web application attacks
- Basic pivoting between hosts

It assumes little prior knowledge and holds your hand more than any other cert here. It does **not** go deep on Active Directory, and it will not teach you to write a client-facing deliverable.

### Who it's for

Total beginners, career changers, students, and anyone who wants a confidence-building first win before committing to something harder. If you have been grinding [getting into cybersecurity with CTFs](/career/how-to-get-into-cybersecurity-with-ctfs/) to build prerequisite skills, the eJPT is the natural first certificate to formalize them.

**Pros**
- Genuinely beginner-friendly, low stress, generous time
- Cheap relative to other practical certs
- No report to write — pure hands-on skill check
- Respected vendor, recognized as a legitimate entry credential

**Cons**
- Light on Active Directory
- No report or debrief, so it doesn't prove consulting-style communication
- Weaker standalone resume signal than the PNPT for a pentest role
- Multiple-choice framing feels less "real" than a straight objective-based exam

## PNPT deep dive

### What it is

The **PNPT** — **Practical Network Penetration Tester** by **TCM Security** — is an **intermediate**, fully practical certification built to mirror a real consulting engagement rather than a lab puzzle. This is the differentiator that matters, so read the format carefully.

### The 5-day exam + report + debrief

- **5 days** to compromise a realistic, multi-host network from the outside in.
- **2 additional days** to write a **professional penetration test report** — the same kind of deliverable a paying client would receive.
- A **live ~15-minute debrief** with TCM Security assessors, where you present and defend your findings as you would to a client.

The engagement emphasizes the full kill chain: **OSINT, external attack surface, phishing, initial access, and then internal Active Directory exploitation and pivoting**. You start with reconnaissance and end with a domain compromise and a written report — exactly the arc of a real internal pentest.

### Who it's for

People who already have fundamentals (the eJPT, solid CTF practice, or equivalent hands-on time) and want a credential that looks and feels like the job. If your goal is a pentest role, the PNPT is the closest thing to a real consulting deliverable you can put on a resume at this level.

**Pros**
- Report + live debrief mirror an actual engagement — rare at this price
- Real Active Directory exploitation and pivoting, not a token mention
- Strong, differentiated resume signal for pentest roles
- Includes a free retake (verify), which lowers the risk
- OSINT and phishing coverage reflects how real engagements actually start

**Cons**
- Harder — a real step up from the eJPT
- The report is graded and can sink an otherwise good technical performance
- The live debrief intimidates people who have never presented findings
- Slightly more expensive, though still excellent value

## Key differences that actually matter

Ignore the marketing bullet lists. These five differences decide which cert is right for you.

1. **Report and live debrief.** This is the single biggest gap. The PNPT forces you to *write and defend* a professional report; the eJPT never asks you to write anything. Reporting is what hiring managers complain juniors can't do — so the PNPT proves a skill the eJPT simply doesn't test.
2. **Active Directory depth.** The eJPT barely touches AD. The PNPT makes internal AD exploitation and pivoting a core objective. Since AD is what most real internal engagements revolve around, this is a meaningful capability difference, not a footnote.
3. **The difficulty jump.** The eJPT is deliberately gentle. The PNPT is a genuine intermediate exam with a full week of hands-on plus reporting. Going straight to the PNPT with zero fundamentals is a common way to waste a voucher.
4. **Resume signal.** Both are respected, but for a pentest job the PNPT says more. "Compromised a network, wrote the report, defended it live" reads as job-ready in a way "passed a beginner lab quiz" does not.
5. **Price.** The eJPT (~$249, or bundled with an INE subscription — verify) is cheaper than the PNPT (~$400 voucher — verify). On a budget, the eJPT gets you a legitimate practical credential for less.

## Which should you take?

Decision by scenario:

- **Total beginner, new to hacking:** Start with the **eJPT**. Build confidence, learn the workflow, avoid the stress of a graded report before you're ready. Then progress to the PNPT.
- **Some experience (CTFs, a home lab, basic exploitation):** You can consider **jumping straight to the PNPT**, but do an honest gut check on your Active Directory comfort first. If AD and pivoting feel shaky, the eJPT is a cheap warm-up.
- **Aiming for a pentest job specifically:** The **PNPT** is the stronger signal because of the report and debrief. Ideally do the eJPT first, then the PNPT — but the PNPT is the one that belongs on the resume for the role.
- **On a tight budget:** The **eJPT** delivers a respected practical credential for the lowest cost. Take it now, save for the PNPT later.

The honest default for most people: **eJPT, then PNPT.** You get a cheap confidence win, then a job-ready credential, in a sensible difficulty curve.

## What comes after

Once you have the PNPT, you have proven you can run an engagement and write it up. The natural next steps push depth and prestige:

- **HTB CPTS** — Hack The Box's Certified Penetration Testing Specialist is a long, brutally thorough practical exam with a mandatory report. It's an excellent bridge between the PNPT and OSCP. See our [HTB CPTS review](/certifications/htb-cpts-review-2026/) for the full breakdown.
- **OSCP** — still the most recognized name in the offensive market. It's harder and more expensive, and the PNPT's report discipline plus AD practice is genuinely good preparation. Map out the path with our [OSCP roadmap](/certifications/oscp-preparation-roadmap-2026/).

## FAQ

**Should I take the eJPT or PNPT first?**
Take the eJPT first if you're a beginner — it's cheaper, gentler, and has no report. Move to the PNPT once your fundamentals are solid. If you already have real hands-on experience and are comfortable with Active Directory, you can start with the PNPT.

**Is the eJPT enough to get a job?**
The eJPT alone is a legitimate entry credential but is usually not enough on its own for a dedicated pentest role. Pair it with a portfolio — CTF writeups, rooted boxes, a home lab — or follow it with the PNPT for a much stronger signal.

**Is the PNPT worth it?**
For most people aiming at a pentest career, yes. The graded report, live debrief, and real Active Directory exploitation mirror an actual engagement more closely than almost anything at this price and level.

**PNPT vs OSCP — which is better?**
They're different, not strictly ranked. The OSCP has broader name recognition with employers and HR filters; the PNPT better simulates a real engagement with its OSINT-to-report workflow. Many people do the PNPT first, then the OSCP. Verify current OSCP format and pricing before committing.

**How hard is the eJPT?**
It's beginner-friendly by design — generous time, low stress, and answers you derive by actually exploiting the lab. If you understand basic networking and enumeration, it's very achievable as a first practical exam.

**Do I need a report for the eJPT?**
No. The eJPT has no written report; you answer dynamic multiple-choice questions based on what you find in the lab. The PNPT is where report-writing (and defending it in a live debrief) becomes part of the grade.
