This **CyberSecurity Elite** **CompTIA Security+ study guide** is the plan we wish someone had handed us before sitting the SY0-701 exam: an honest read on whether the cert is worth it, a domain-by-domain breakdown, a realistic week-by-week schedule, and the exact free and paid resources that get people over the line. Security+ is the single most common entry-level cybersecurity certification, and it is very passable in four to eight weeks if you study the right things in the right order.

> **Confirm current details before you buy.** CompTIA rotates exam versions, adjusts question counts, and changes pricing, bundles, and retake policies regularly. Every figure below is accurate as of 2026 but should be treated as a ballpark. Always verify the current exam objectives, format, and voucher cost on CompTIA's official site before scheduling.

## Is Security+ worth it?

Let's be blunt: Security+ is a **baseline, HR-filter certification** — and that is exactly why it matters. It is not a hacking cert, it will not teach you to root a box, and it will not, by itself, make you a penetration tester. What it does is get your resume past automated filters and human screeners for entry-level roles: SOC analyst, junior security analyst, GRC and compliance roles, and a wide range of defensive and IT-adjacent positions.

Two things make it disproportionately valuable for beginners:

- **It is broad.** Security+ forces you to learn the vocabulary of the entire field — cryptography, network security, identity, risk, incident response, governance. That shared language is the foundation everything else builds on.
- **It is DoD 8140/8570 IAT Level II approved.** This is the real reason so many U.S. government, defense contractor, and defense-adjacent job postings list it as a hard requirement. If you want to work in or around government, Security+ is often non-negotiable.

If your goal is offensive security, Security+ is still a smart first step for the vocabulary and the HR filter, but plan to follow it with hands-on work. For the wider landscape, see our [best cybersecurity certifications guide](/certifications/best-cybersecurity-certifications-2026/), which ranks certs by goal rather than by vendor.

## Security+ (SY0-701) exam at a glance

The current version is **SY0-701**, launched in November 2023; the previous SY0-601 has retired, so make sure any material you buy targets 701.

| Attribute | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Version | SY0-701 (current; SY0-601 retired) |
| Format | Multiple-choice plus performance-based questions (PBQs) |
| Number of questions | Up to 90 |
| Time | 90 minutes |
| Passing score | 750 on a 100–900 scale |
| Cost | Around $400 per voucher (verify; discounts and bundles exist) |
| Validity | 3 years, renewable via continuing education (CEUs) |
| Delivery | Pearson VUE test center or online proctored |

A few notes on the numbers. The passing score of 750 is **not** a straightforward 83% — the exam is scaled, so you don't need to nail every question. There are "up to" 90 questions because the count varies slightly by exam form. And that roughly $400 voucher can often be reduced through CompTIA vouchers, student pricing, retake bundles, or the CertMaster training bundle, so shop around before paying full price.

## The five SY0-701 domains

The exam is built from five domains with fixed weightings. Study to the weightings — this is the single biggest efficiency win available to you.

| # | Domain | Weight | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | General Security Concepts | 12% | Core terms, control types, CIA triad, cryptography basics, change management |
| 2 | Threats, Vulnerabilities & Mitigations | 22% | Threat actors, attack types, malware, vulnerabilities, mitigation techniques |
| 3 | Security Architecture | 18% | Secure network/cloud design, zero trust, resilience, data protection |
| 4 | Security Operations | 28% | Hardening, monitoring, identity/access, incident response, digital forensics |
| 5 | Security Program Management & Oversight | 20% | Governance, risk management, third-party risk, compliance, audits |

### Where to spend your time

**Domain 4 (Security Operations, 28%)** is the heaviest by a wide margin and the most hands-on-flavored. This is where most PBQs live — log analysis, incident response steps, hardening tasks, identity and access management. If you're short on time, this is the domain to over-prepare.

**Domain 2 (Threats, Vulnerabilities & Mitigations, 22%)** is the second-largest and the most "fun" for security-minded learners. Expect a lot of attack-type identification: know the difference between a replay attack and an on-path attack, between phishing variants, between the malware families. Rote familiarity pays off here.

**Domains 3 and 5 (18% and 20%)** together account for 38% of the exam and are heavy on concepts and terminology rather than technical mechanics. Don't neglect Domain 5 — candidates from technical backgrounds routinely underestimate governance, risk, and compliance and lose easy points there.

## A 6-week study plan

This assumes roughly 1–2 focused hours on weekdays and a longer session on weekends. If you have solid IT fundamentals (Network+ level or equivalent experience), **4–8 weeks** is realistic. A complete beginner should stretch this to 8–12 weeks and go slower on the networking concepts.

| Week | Focus | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Domain 1 + start Domain 2 | Watch Professor Messer's opening sections; build a notes doc; learn core vocabulary and control types |
| 2 | Finish Domain 2 (Threats) | Memorize attack types and malware families with flashcards; first easy practice questions |
| 3 | Domain 3 (Architecture) | Zero trust, secure design, cloud, resilience; connect concepts to real systems you've used |
| 4 | Domain 4 (Operations) — part 1 | Hardening, monitoring, IAM; start doing PBQ-style drag-and-drop practice |
| 5 | Domain 4 part 2 + Domain 5 | Incident response, forensics, then governance, risk, and compliance; first full-length practice exam |
| 6 | Review + practice exams | Take 2–3 timed practice exams; drill weak areas; review missed questions until you know *why* |

Rule of thumb for scheduling the real exam: **book it once you consistently score 85%+ on quality practice exams** (like Jason Dion's) across multiple attempts. Practice exams that are harder than the real thing are a feature, not a bug — they build a buffer.

## Best study resources

Lead with the free option — for most people it is genuinely all you need for the theory.

- **Professor Messer's SY0-701 course (free).** A complete video course on YouTube plus free study notes, and the single most-recommended free resource in the entire community. Watch it start to finish, take notes, and use it as your spine.
- **Jason Dion practice exams (Udemy, paid).** The most popular practice tests. Their job is to expose your weak spots and get you comfortable with the question style and time pressure. Frequently on sale.
- **CompTIA CertMaster Learn/Labs (official, paid).** The official interactive courseware and labs. Pricier, but the labs are useful if you learn by doing and want PBQ-style practice from the source.
- **"Get Certified Get Ahead" by Darril Gibson (book).** The classic Security+ study book, well-organized with strong end-of-chapter questions. Great for readers who retain more from text than video.

You do not need all of these. A proven, cheap combo is **Professor Messer (free) + one set of Jason Dion practice exams**. Add the book if you prefer reading; add CertMaster if you want official labs.

## How to beat the PBQs

Performance-based questions scare people more than they should. Here is the reality: **PBQs are simulations** — drag-and-drop matching, configuration screens, log-analysis tasks, or "click the vulnerable setting" interfaces. They typically appear first in the exam, and there are usually only a handful of them.

Tactics that work:

- **Skip and come back.** If a PBQ isn't obvious, flag it, move on to the multiple-choice questions, and return at the end. Don't let one hard simulation eat 15 minutes and your confidence in the first five minutes.
- **They're weighted, but not all-or-nothing.** Partial credit is generally awarded, so attempt every part even if you're unsure of the whole.
- **Practice the format, not just the facts.** Reading about firewall rules is not the same as dragging them into order under a timer. CertMaster Labs and Dion's PBQ-style questions build the muscle memory.
- **The content is mostly Domain 4.** Hardening, IAM, incident response, and log reading show up again and again. Prepare those areas specifically as "I might have to *do* this," not just recognize it.

## Exam-day tips

- **Arrive (or log in) early.** Online proctoring requires a room scan, ID check, and a clear desk. Build in buffer time and read the environment requirements the day before.
- **Do the PBQs last if any give you trouble** — bank the fast multiple-choice points first.
- **Use the full 90 minutes.** Flag anything uncertain and review at the end. Rushing loses more points than second-guessing does.
- **Read questions for the *best* answer.** Security+ loves "most likely" and "best" phrasing where several options are technically valid but one is clearly superior. Eliminate the obviously wrong two, then choose the strongest of the rest.
- **Watch for absolutes.** Answers with "always" or "never" are often traps.

## What to do after Security+

Security+ proves you know the language. The next step is proving you can **do** the work — and that is where hands-on practice separates candidates.

- **Get your hands dirty immediately.** Follow our [hands-on roadmap for getting into cybersecurity with CTFs](/career/how-to-get-into-cybersecurity-with-ctfs/) to turn theory into practical skill. Capture-the-flag challenges and lab boxes teach the "how" that a multiple-choice exam never can.
- **Plan the career path, not just the next cert.** Our guide to [breaking into cybersecurity](/career/breaking-into-cybersecurity-2026/) covers resumes, home labs, and the realistic first-job funnel beyond the certification itself.
- **Layer a second cert to your goal.** Defensive-minded? CySA+ or a blue-team path. Offensive-minded? Start building toward the [OSCP preparation roadmap](/certifications/oscp-preparation-roadmap-2026/) — a hands-on, exploit-and-report exam that is a world apart from Security+ and a serious credential for pentest roles.

The pattern that works: **cert for the HR filter, portfolio for the hiring manager.** Security+ opens the door; a GitHub of writeups, a few rooted boxes, and a home lab get you the offer.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to study for Security+?**
For someone with some IT background, four to eight weeks of consistent study is realistic. A complete beginner should plan for eight to twelve weeks and spend extra time on networking fundamentals.

**What is the passing score for Security+?**
750 on a scaled range of 100 to 900. Because the exam is scaled, this does not translate to a fixed percentage, but you do not need a perfect score to pass.

**Is Professor Messer enough to pass Security+?**
For most people, Professor Messer's free video course and notes cover the theory completely. Pairing it with a set of practice exams, such as Jason Dion's, to drill the question format is the most reliable low-cost approach.

**How much does the Security+ exam cost?**
A voucher is around $400 as of 2026, but prices change and discounts exist through CompTIA vouchers, student pricing, retake bundles, and the CertMaster bundle. Verify the current price on CompTIA's official site.

**Is Security+ worth it in 2026?**
Yes for entry-level defensive, SOC, and government-adjacent roles, where it is a common HR requirement and is DoD 8140/8570 IAT Level II approved. It is a knowledge cert, not a hands-on hacking cert, so pair it with practical skills.

**Should I take Network+ before Security+?**
It helps but is not required. Network+ builds networking fundamentals that make Security+ easier, but many people go straight to Security+ and simply spend extra time on the networking concepts.
